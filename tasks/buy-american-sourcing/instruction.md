The law doesn't pin down a few things, so here is how to handle them. Outlay means the cash we actually spend. For a purchased part that is its price, its freight, and whatever duty we really pay. For an in-house part it is direct cost plus overhead. A US build also carries the assembly cost, which an import does not. Multiply each line's per-unit outlay by its quantity, then add the one-time fees for the newly qualified vendors.

Evaluating offers works differently from outlay. Consider every way a line could be built as if it were a separate offer, and price each one at its per-unit cost with all duty included, whether or not a duty-free certificate was issued. Vendor qualification plays no part in that comparison. Only where the evaluation permits it may a line go out foreign.
